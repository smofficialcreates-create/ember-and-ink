Welcome to today's literary deep dive! If you have ever pondered the meaning of life—or questioned whether it has one at all—you need to read *The Stranger* by Albert Camus. Published in 1942, this foundational text of existential literature introduces us to Meursault, an emotionally detached protagonist whose indifference shocks society just as much as his sudden, violent crime.

At its core, the novel is a masterclass in the philosophy of the absurd. The story is split into two halves: Meursault's life leading up to a senseless murder on a scorching Algerian beach, and his subsequent trial. Camus does not offer neat moral lessons or comforting resolutions. Instead, he presents a character who refuses to play society's game of pretense, remaining radically honest about the meaningless nature of existence right up until his final hours.

This book is an absolute must-read for fans of philosophical fiction, classic literature, and readers who enjoy thought-provoking narratives that linger long after the final page. If you loved Dostoevsky's *Notes from Underground* or Kafka's *The Metamorphosis*, *The Stranger* will fit right into your bookshelf and challenge the way you view the world.
