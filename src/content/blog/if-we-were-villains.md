If you are chasing the intoxicating, moody highs of *The Secret History*, look no further than M.L. Rio’s phenomenal debut, *If We Were Villains*. Set within the cloistered, Gothic walls of an elite arts conservatory, this novel immediately draws readers into a world where Shakespearean tragedy bleeds seamlessly into real life. It’s atmospheric, intensely theatrical, and impossible to put down from the very first page.

The story follows Oliver Marks, one of seven brilliant acting students whose lives are defined by the works of the Bard. On and off the stage, they play specific archetypes: the hero, the tyrant, the temptress, the ingénue. But when the lines between their stage roles and reality blur, a harmless rivalry spirals into deadly betrayal. A decade later, Oliver is finally released from prison, and Detective Colborne wants to know the truth about what really happened the night one of their own ended up dead.

This book is an absolute must-read for fans of Dark Academia, high-stakes psychological thrillers, and anyone who appreciates lush, literary prose. If you love morally grey characters, intense friendships, and academic settings dripping in pretension and secrets, this story will consume you entirely. It rewards careful readers with clever allusions and an ending that lingers long after you close the cover.
